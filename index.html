About Us

Why Theoretical & Physical Chemistry?

At the heart of every material, every drug, every chemical transformation lies a quantum-mechanical reality we can barely see — but can rigorously describe. Theoretical and physical chemistry is the discipline that builds the language to do so.

From the way electrons dance between atoms to the collective behavior of millions of molecules in a condensed phase, our group seeks to understand the rules that govern chemistry from first principles. We use the power of quantum mechanics, statistical mechanics, and computational simulation not merely as tools, but as windows into a molecular world invisible to the eye.

What makes this work exciting is its universality: the same framework that explains why water expands when it freezes can illuminate how a protein misfolds, or how a catalyst lowers a reaction's energy barrier. Theory and simulation allow us to ask "why?" — and to design molecules with purpose before they are ever synthesized.

Research Focus

Main Research Interests

  • ⚛️

    Quantum Chemistry & Electronic Structure

    Development and application of ab initio and density functional methods to study chemical bonding, reaction mechanisms, and excited-state phenomena in molecular systems.

  • 🌊

    Statistical Mechanics & Thermodynamics

    Connecting microscopic molecular interactions to macroscopic thermodynamic properties through statistical mechanical formalisms, partition functions, and free-energy methods.

  • 💻

    Molecular Dynamics & Monte Carlo Simulation

    Classical and enhanced-sampling simulation techniques to study protein folding, ion solvation, phase transitions, and complex fluid behavior at the atomistic level.

  • 🔬

    Spectroscopy & Optical Properties

    Theoretical modeling of electronic, vibrational, and rotational spectra; coupling simulation with experimental spectroscopic data to characterize molecular structure and dynamics.

  • 🧬

    Drug–Target Interactions & Biomolecular Systems

    Applying physical chemistry frameworks — docking, free-energy perturbation, and QM/MM — to explore how small molecules interact with biological macromolecules.
